# 📘 Retrospectives

:::tip

## Sprint Retrospectives & Continuous Improvement

**What is it?** 
A structured team facilitation skill used at the end of iteration cycles to evaluate team dynamics, engineering workflows, and delivery bottlenecks, converting raw qualitative feedback into concrete, owner-assigned improvement items.

#### 📚 Related Theory & Practice References
* Theory — Agile delivery governance, agile metrics, and team dynamics.
* Practice — Jira workspace tracking, confluence documentation, and squad delivery workflows.
:::

## 🧭 When is it Used?

| When to Use | When Not to Use |
| :--- | :--- |
| • At the end of every scheduled sprint or iteration cycle. • Following major milestone deliveries, product releases, or prolonged incident recovery periods. • When team velocity is dropping or friction around engineering workflows is increasing. | • Ad-hoc daily standups or tactical bug triage sessions. • Individual developer performance reviews or disciplinary evaluations. |

---

## 💡 What Value Does It Bring?

* **Prevents Retro Fatigue:** Ensures meetings result in real, tracked action items rather than unstructured venting sessions that lead to no change.
* **Drives Continuous Evolution:** systematically eliminates workflow bottlenecks, CI/CD friction, and communication gaps sprint by sprint.
* **Fosters Psychological Safety:** Creates a blame-free environment focused on fixing systemic process failures rather than targeting individuals.

---

## 🧠 Core Concepts

| Concept / Mechanism | Plain English Explanation | Execution Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prime Directive Alignment** | Establishing that everyone did the best job they could given what they knew at the time. | Eliminates defensiveness and opens the team to honest, constructive reflections. |
| **Theme Grouping** | Clustering dozens of individual feedback cards into unified, manageable categories. | Focuses discussion on high-impact macro trends rather than getting lost in isolated complaints. |
| **Actionable Accountability** | Assigning single owners and tracked Jira tasks to every single improvement item generated. | Guarantees that retro outcomes are executed rather than forgotten in static meeting notes. |

---

## 📋 Common Patterns

1. **Phase 1: Preparation & Data Gathering** — Set up the retro board 24-48 hours prior, pull sprint delivery metrics, and review the status of previous action items.
2. **Phase 2: Silent Brainstorming & Grouping** — Give the team silent time to log cards under "What Went Well", "What Needs Improvement", and "Ideas", then group related themes.
3. **Phase 3: Voting & Action Item Commitment** — Vote on priority topics, discuss constructive solutions, and assign 1 to 3 tracked action items with clear owners.

---

## 🛠️ Practical Tips & Templates

> **Template / Artifact Example:** Sprint Retrospective Confluence or Board Template.

```markdown
# Sprint [X] Retrospective Summary

## 1. Review of Previous Action Items
- [x] [COMPLETED] Add automated linter check to PR pipeline (Owner: Alex)
- [ ] [IN PROGRESS] Update staging database seed script (Owner: Sam)

## 2. Feedback Summary
- **🟢 What Went Well:** Quick PR turnaround times; clear TRDs.
- **🔴 What Needs Improvement:** Staging environment instability; missing acceptance criteria on incoming tickets.
- **💡 Ideas & Experiments:** Implement a 15-minute daily blocker clearance drop-in call.

## 3. Actionable Commitments for Next Sprint
| Action Item | Type | Owner | Target Ticket / Sprint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Add DoR checklist enforcement before pulling tickets | Process | Technical Analyst | Sprint [X+1] |
| Set up alert notifications for staging build failures | Tooling | DevOps / Sam | Jira: PLAT-402 |

```

:::warning

### Pitfalls & Edge Cases

* **Common Mistake:** Accumulating a giant list of 10+ action items that nobody owns or completes, destroying team faith in the retro process.
* **Pro Tip:** Strictly cap action items at 1 to 3 per sprint. It is far better to execute *one* meaningful workflow change than to log ten ideas that get ignored.
:::
