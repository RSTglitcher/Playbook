:::tip

**Operational Purpose:** Drive agile execution within cross-functional engineering squads by refining requirements, managing work-in-progress (WIP), clearing technical blockers, and maintaining delivery momentum from sprint planning through deployment.

:::

---

## 1. Context & Application Trigger

* **When to perform:** Ongoing daily execution within an embedded product engineering squad, sprint planning cycles, daily standups, and feature rollouts.
* **Inputs needed:** Product backlog items, refined user stories, sprint goals, capacity models, and delivery telemetry (burndown/velocity).
* **Expected Output:** Predictable sprint delivery, zero unhandled technical blockers, up-to-date board status, and verified feature releases.

---

## 2. Standard Operating Procedure

1. **Phase 1: Sprint Planning & Goal Alignment**
   * Partner with the Product Owner (PO) and Engineering Lead to define realistic sprint goals based on team velocity and capacity.
   * Ensure candidate tickets meet the **Definition of Ready (DoR)** prior to pulling them into the active sprint.
   * Confirm dependencies across other squads or platform teams are identified and scheduled.

2. **Phase 2: Daily Execution & Blocker Clearance**
   * Facilitate or participate in daily standups focusing on flow: *What is blocked? What is close to finishing? Are we on track for the sprint goal?*
   * Actively clear non-engineering friction (e.g., missing API contracts, pending design assets, third-party credentials).
   * Monitor Work-In-Progress (WIP) limits to prevent context switching and bottlenecks in code review or QA.

3. **Phase 3: Mid-Sprint Tracking & Scope Management**
   * Track sprint burndown and scope changes. If unplanned high-priority work enters the sprint, negotiate trade-offs to drop equivalent story points.
   * Ensure pull requests (PRs) are progressing through review and staging environments without getting stuck.

4. **Phase 4: Release Verification & Definition of Done (DoD)**
   * Verify that completed work satisfies acceptance criteria and meets the **Definition of Done (DoD)**.
   * Coordinate release notes, staging sign-offs, and feature flag toggles with DevOps/Product.

## 3. Key Artifacts & Templates

### Squad Delivery Checklist & Metrics

```text
# Sprint Delivery Checklist

## Pre-Sprint / Planning
- [ ] Sprint goals clearly articulated and linked to top backlog epics
- [ ] All sprint tickets estimated and meet Definition of Ready (DoR)
- [ ] Team capacity accounts for planned PTO, on-call duties, and support work

## In-Sprint Execution
- [ ] Daily standup focused on unblocking WIP rather than status reporting
- [ ] PR turnaround times monitored (<24h target)
- [ ] Mid-sprint scope changes explicitly negotiated and logged

## Release & Post-Sprint
- [ ] Acceptance criteria validated in staging environment
- [ ] Release notes published to stakeholder channels
- [ ] Sprint retro items logged for continuous improvement

```

---

## 4. Quality Checklist & Verification

* [ ] Sprint commitments are based on historical velocity rather than aspirational targets
* [ ] WIP limits are respected to maximize ticket throughput
* [ ] External dependencies are identified and tracked before work begins
* [ ] Stakeholders have visibility into delivery progress via updated boards and dashboards

---

:::warning

## Pitfalls & Edge Cases

* **Common Mistake:** Allowing scope creep mid-sprint without dropping lower-priority items, leading to missed sprint goals and team burnout.
* **Pro Tip:** Focus on completing tickets sequentially rather than starting multiple items simultaneously. Pushing a single ticket to "Done" provides more value than five tickets sitting at 80% complete.

:::
