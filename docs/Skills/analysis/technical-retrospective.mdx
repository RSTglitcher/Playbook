# Sprint Retrospectives & Continuous Improvement

:::tip

**Operational Purpose:** Lead reflective team sessions at the end of an iteration to evaluate team dynamics, engineering workflows, and delivery bottlenecks—converting raw feedback into actionable, owner-assigned improvement items.

:::

---

## 1. Context & Application Trigger

* **When to perform:** At the end of each sprint cycle, following a major feature release, or after a prolonged incident recovery period.
* **Inputs needed:** Completed sprint metrics (velocity, roll-over work, bug counts), team feedback entries (What went well, What didn't, Action items), and previous retro action items.
* **Expected Output:** A completed Retrospective board/document, celebrated achievements, and 1–3 high-priority, owner-assigned action items logged in the backlog for the next sprint.

---

## 2. Standard Operating Procedure

1. **Phase 1: Session Preparation & Data Gathering (Pre-Retro)**
   * Set up an async retro board (e.g., Miro, EasyRetro, or Jira Retro) 24–48 hours before the meeting.
   * Pull sprint delivery data (e.g., completed story points, unplanned scope additions, PR review turnaround times).
   * Review the status of action items committed during the *previous* retrospective.

2. **Phase 2: Setting the Stage & Psychological Safety**
   * Reiterate Norm Kerth's Prime Directive: *"Regardless of what we discover, we understand and truly believe that everyone did the best job they could..."*
   * Review previous action items: Celebrate completed items and acknowledge uncompleted ones without finger-pointing.

3. **Phase 3: Silent Brainstorming & Grouping**
   * Give the team 5–7 minutes of silent writing time to populate cards under three primary pillars:
     * **What Went Well:** Successes, smooth deployments, great collaboration.
     * **What Needs Improvement:** Bottlenecks, unclear specs, environment downtime, process friction.
     * **Ideas & Questions:** Proposed experiments, questions about new tooling or workflows.
   * Group duplicate or closely related cards into unified themes.

4. **Phase 4: Voting, Discussion & Action Item Generation**
   * Allow team members 3–5 votes to prioritize the themes that matter most.
   * Discuss top-voted topics starting from the highest vote count. Focus discussions on **actionable solutions** rather than venting.
   * Convert key insights into explicit, measurable **Action Items** with dedicated owners and target delivery dates.

## 3. Key Artifacts & Templates

### Retrospective Board & Action Item Template

```text
# Sprint [X] Retrospective

**Date:** YYYY-MM-DD  
**Facilitator:** Technical Analyst  
**Participants:** Engineering Team, QA, Product Owner  

---

## 1. Review of Previous Action Items
- [x] **[COMPLETED]** Add automated linter check to PR pipeline (Owner: Alex)
- [ ] **[IN PROGRESS]** Update staging database seed script (Owner: Sam)

---

## 2. Feedback Summary

### 🟢 What Went Well
- Quick turnaround on PR code reviews this sprint.
- The new Technical Requirement Document format made API integration much clearer.

### 🔴 What Needs Improvement
- Staging environment was down for 2 days due to broken migrations.
- Several tickets entered the sprint without explicit acceptance criteria.

### 💡 Ideas & Experiments
- Try adding a automated smoke-test check before merging to staging.
- Implement a 15-minute daily "blocker clearance" drop-in call.

---

## 3. Actionable Commitments for Next Sprint

| Action Item | Type | Owner | Target Ticket / Sprint |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Add DoR checklist enforcement before pulling tickets into sprint | Process | Technical Analyst | Sprint [X+1] |
| Set up alert notifications for staging environment build failures | Tooling | DevOps / Sam | Jira: PLAT-402 |

```

---

## 4. Quality Checklist & Verification

* [ ] Every action item has a single explicit owner (no "unowned" or group-owned tasks)
* [ ] Total action items are capped at 1–3 items per sprint to avoid overwhelming the team
* [ ] Action items are logged as actual Jira/backlog tasks rather than buried in meeting notes
* [ ] Retro feedback focuses on processes and workflows rather than personal critiques

---

:::warning

## Pitfalls & Edge Cases

* **Common Mistake:** Creating a huge list of 10+ action items that nobody completes, leading to "retro fatigue" where the team feels nothing changes.
* **Pro Tip:** Keep action items tightly focused. It is far better to execute **one** impactful process change every sprint than to record ten suggestions that get ignored.

:::
